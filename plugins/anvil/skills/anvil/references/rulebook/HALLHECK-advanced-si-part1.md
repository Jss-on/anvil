1. **Impedance/eps_eff vs frequency (microstrip dispersion)** — x: f 1–100 GHz (log), y: eps_eff(f) from eq.3-37 vs quasi-TEM constant; second panel: % delay error vs f for the actual line length; pass: error < 1% up to f_max (Fig.3-15, 3-16). Corners: min/max h and w from stackup tolerance.
2. **TDR impedance profile** — x: time (ns), y: Z (ohm) via eq.3-113 from a 50-ohm step source (rise 9–35 ps); expected: flat plateau at Z0 for 2*tau_d, dips = shunt C, spikes = series L (Fig.3-43..3-48); pass: plateau within Z0 tolerance; extract L/C from spike/dip areas (eq.3-114/3-115). Note reactive elements slow the edge and blur later readings.
3. **Bounce/lattice or SPICE step response at source and load** — x: t (ns, 0–5), y: V; sweep t_r = 10/250/500/750 ps vs tau_d (Fig.3-31, 3-32, 3-37, 3-38); pass: no ring-back into the threshold region (v_IH/v_IL) and monotonic edges; underdriven (stair-step) vs overdriven (ringing) signatures identify Rs vs Z0 mismatch.
4. **Multi-drop topology waveforms** — per receiver, x: t, y: V; check symmetry of legs (length, Z0, load); pass: monotonic through threshold (Fig.3-34, 3-36).
5. **Coupled-pair even/odd waveforms** — drive both lines in even then odd mode; x: t (0–5 ns), y: V at z = 0 and z = l; compare SLEM prediction vs fully coupled sim (Fig.4-13, 4-27); pass: delays td_even/td_odd and levels match within a few %.

7. **R(f) and L(f) of the conductor** — x: f 10 MHz–100 GHz (log), y1: R (ohm/m) rising as sqrt(f) above f_(delta=t), y2: L_total falling from L_ext + L_int(low f) to L_ext (Fig.5-6, 5-11, 6-25a,b); pass: R/L pair consistent (L_int = R_ac/omega) — causality sanity.

## 5. Pitfalls, failure modes, review checklist

- A single lumped LC per line is only valid when its resonance is far above f_sim; cascade Ns >= 10*l*sqrt(epsr)/(t_r*c) segments (p.78-79).
- Impedance formulas without sigma or mu assume a perfect conductor — the inductance they yield is the external inductance only (p.217).
- Do not use eq.3-36b outside 1 < epsr <= 16, 0.25 <= w/h <= 6, nor the IPC stripline forms outside 0.1 < w/h < 2, t/h < 0.25, 1 < epsr < 15 (p.85-86).
- Thick dielectrics (h ~ 25 mil) lose quasi-TEM accuracy above ~8 GHz on 12-in microstrips; thin (2.5 mil) are fine to ~80 GHz (p.89-90).
- Uniform-charge hand formulas over-estimate microstrip Z0 by 5–15% versus a field solver (Table 3-1).
- Multi-drop nets: unequal leg lengths/impedances/loads create non-monotonic, ringing edges; junction into two equal legs needs Z_leg = 2*Z_base (p.125-129).
- Slow edges hide reflections only when t_r > 2*tau_d; faster silicon later will expose them (p.129).
- Series inductance at terminations (bond wires, lead frames, vias) makes a positive spike; shunt pad/via capacitance makes a dip; both slow the edge (p.130-134).
- TDR readings after a reactive discontinuity are less accurate because the element filters the step (p.140).
- SLEM is exact only for two lines; for three or more use it only when s/h > 1 and only for same-direction signals; final sign-off needs fully coupled simulation (p.165).
- Capacitance matrix diagonal is total capacitance (self + all mutuals) with negative off-diagonals — do not treat diagonals as capacitance to ground (p.151).
- Closed-form FEXT is invalid once td_even - td_odd > t_r; the pulse widens instead of growing (p.176-177).
- Microstrip FEXT polarity is opposite to the aggressor (K_L > K_C); striplines have essentially no FEXT but still have NEXT and pattern-dependent Z0 (p.159-160, 174).
- Guard traces need dense ground stitching; at multi-Gb/s the required via pitch is impractical and an under-stitched guard radiates (p.193-194).
- Thinning the dielectric to cut crosstalk lowers Z0 and is bounded by manufacturable thickness (Table 4-4).
- Above f_(delta=t) the resistance rises as sqrt(f); a dc-only R model under-predicts loss; use root-sum-square R_dc/R_ac (p.208-209).
- Neglecting the reference-plane return resistance under-estimates microstrip ac loss by the l/(6h) term (p.210-211).
- Hammerstad roughness factor saturates at 2 and under-predicts rough-foil loss above ~5 GHz; use only for h_RMS < ~2 um (p.225-227, 243).
- Roughness cannot be ignored when the skin depth approaches h_RMS (copper: ~2 um at 1 GHz); check f_r = 1/(pi*mu0*sigma*h_RMS^2) against f_max (p.222, 227).
- Field solvers assume smooth conductors — add roughness and internal inductance externally (p.222).
- For the hemisphere/Huray eta and lambda use the permittivity directly under the rough surface, not eps_eff (p.236).
- Internal inductance must fall as R_ac rises (L_int = R_ac/omega); a model with frequency-flat R and L is non-causal (p.216, 224 footnote).
- Frequency-invariant epsr/tan d models produce large amplitude/phase errors above ~1–2 Gb/s, worse for long lines (p.269, 271, 316).
- A physical dielectric has eps' falling and tan d rising with f (below its pole); check Kramers–Kronig/Debye consistency (p.270-274).
- Watch for FR4 dielectric resonances as low as 30 GHz: narrowband loss peak plus phase-velocity increase (p.264).
- Infinite-pole model is only for dry/nominal (<= ~50% RH) conditions; FR4 at 95% RH above 10 GHz needs a multipole fit (p.266, 285).
- Humid FR4 raises tan d by > 50% (1.57x) but epsr only ~4%; loss budgets must include the humid corner unless the usage model bounds RH (p.280-284).
- Microstrips saturate with moisture in ~7 days; striplines take ~5 months but are not immune; boards absorb moisture while powered down (p.283-284).
- Routing at 0/90 deg to the board edge maximizes fiber-weave skew; rotate >= 5–10 deg (45 deg best) (p.275-277).
- Fiber weave can give d_eps_eff up to 0.4 on microstrip; a 10-in pair with d_eps_eff 0.23 converts 100% to common mode near 10 GHz (p.276, 312).
- Differential pairs do not beat widely spaced single-ended lines on crosstalk for equal board area (p.300-302).
- Differential impedance tolerance is worse than single-ended because etch/plating changes L_M and C_M (p.305).
- Z_diff = 2*Z_odd and Z_common = Z_even/2 are only valid for weak inter-pair coupling (p.304).
- Intra-pair length mismatch dl converts differential to common mode: 100% at f = vp/(2*dl) (6 mm in FR4 → 12.5 GHz); operating past the ACCM peak mis-latches bits (p.308-310).
- Keep Fourier-transform conventions (a, b) consistent between tools when moving between time and frequency domain (p.318).
