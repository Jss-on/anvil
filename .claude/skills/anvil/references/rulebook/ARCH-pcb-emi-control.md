### F. Formulas (plain ASCII; symbols and units as defined)

- F-1 Square-wave harmonic amplitude: `A_n = 1/n`, n odd (Eq. 2.2, p.15).
- F-2 Trapezoid spectral envelope corners: `f1 = 1/(pi*d)`, `f2 = 1/(pi*t)`; −20 dB/dec between, −40 dB/dec above; d = pulse width (s), t = rise/fall time (s) (Fig. 2-4/6-2).
- F-3 NRZ fundamental: `f0 = R_bit/2` (p.17).
- F-4 Capacitor / inductor impedance: `Xc = 1/(2*pi*f*C)`, `XL = 2*pi*f*L` (Eqs. 2.1, 9.1, 9.2).
- F-5 Half-wave resonance of a conductor: `f = c/(2*L)`; 30 cm → ≈500 MHz (p.19).
- F-6 Rectangular cavity modes: `f_mnp = (1/(2*sqrt(mu*eps))) * sqrt((m/a)^2 + (n/b)^2 + (p/c)^2)`, only one of m,n,p zero (Eqs. 2.3, 12.2). Plane-pair (p = 0) form used for PCB cavity (Eq. 8.4, reconstructed).
- F-7 Faraday: `V = -A * dB/dt` for a loop of area A small vs wavelength (Eq. 3.2, p.26).
- F-8 Square loop: `L = (2*mu0*a/pi) * (ln(2*p/(1+sqrt(2))) - 2 + sqrt(2))`, p = a/r0 (Eq. 3.9).
- F-9 Equilateral triangle loop: `L ≈ (3*mu0*s/(2*pi)) * (ln(s/a) - 1.405)` (Eq. 3.12).
- F-10 Wire pair: `L' ≈ (mu0/pi) * acosh(d/(2*a))` H/m, a << d (Eq. 3.14).
- F-11 Bar resistance: `R = l/(sigma*w*t)` (Eq. 3.20).
- F-12 Small-loop field: `E_theta = k*I*S` (Eq. 6.2).
- F-13 Edge rate: `SR = V_swing/t_r` (p.88).
- F-14 I/O demand current (variables from p.126; body illegible): `I_peak ≈ n*C_L*Vcc/t_r`, C_L ≈ 10 pF (CMOS), triangular pulse of width t_r + t_f.
- F-15 Shoot-through current (Eq. 8.2, factor-2 placement illegible): `I_p2 = (2?)*Cpd*m*Vcc/Δt2`, Δt2 = t_r + t_f.
- F-16 Transfer impedance: `Z21(f) = Vo(f)/Is(f) | Io = 0` (Eq. 8.3); from S21 with Z0 = 50 Ω (Eq. 8.5, body illegible).
- F-17 Parallel resonance: `w0 = 1/sqrt(L*C)` (Eq. 12.1).
- F-18 Enclosure worst-case field / SE: see ARCH-113 (Eqs. 12.3, 12.4; OCR-garbled, verify against [12.2]).
- F-19 Guard/fence via pitch: `s ≤ lambda/10 = c/(10*f_max*sqrt(er_eff))` (p.117 states λ/10; 1 in @ 1 GHz).

## 4. Verification procedures & plots

1. **Driver current spectrum vs termination** (ARCH-093, 058): SI simulation of the critical net with IBIS models; x = frequency 100–2000 MHz (two panels 0.1–1, 1–2 GHz), y = harmonic current amplitude (dBµA); one curve per R_series (10, 22, 25, 30, 33, 39 Ω); overlay Δ-reduction plot (0–45 dB). Good: monotonic decrease to a knee; choose knee. Also plot time-domain voltage (check SI) and current.
2. **Trapezoid envelope plot** (ARCH-003): x = log f, y = dB; mark 1/(πd), 1/(πt); overlay measured/simulated harmonics; compare 50 Mb/s@1 ns vs 100 Mb/s@0.8/1/2 ns style sweeps.
3. **Plane-pair transfer impedance S21/Z21** (ARCH-072 to 080): VNA two-port, port 1 centre, port 2 corner (excites most modes < 2 GHz); x = 0–2 GHz linear, y = |S21| dB (0 to −100). Compare cap count, value, mix, plane spacing, lossy mix. Good: lowered envelope, no new 75–200 MHz cross-resonance humps. Full-wave (not SPICE) for simulation.
4. **Split-plane near-field emissions** (ARCH-035, 049): near-field E-field max above board, x = 10–1000 MHz log, y = dBµV/m (0–120); cases: no split, split, 1 cap, 2 caps, caps + 1.5 nH. Good: 2-cap curve tracks no-split to 1 GHz.
5. **CM voltage across split vs stitch-cap distance** (ARCH-040): x = distance 0–2000 mil, y = dB increase (0–20) at 100 MHz. Good: cap within 500 mil.
6. **Board-edge E-field vs trace distance** (ARCH-039): x = distance 0–2000 mil, y = max near E-field along edge (dBµV/m, 60–120); and vs frequency 10–1000 MHz for 10–2000 mil. Good: ≥ 500 mil.
7. **Split impedance vs frequency** (ARCH-047): x = 10–1000 MHz log, y = |Z| 10–10000 Ω log, curves per split width 0.125–1 cm; identify resonance dip (~400–500 MHz for 10 × 12 in).
8. **Heatsink near-field vs contact count** (ARCH-028): FDTD, x = 1e8–1e10 Hz log, y = normalised E-field (dB, 150–200); identify primary resonance and the bands where emissions rise.
9. **I/O filter insertion loss with parasitics** (ARCH-083, 089): x = 1 MHz–1 GHz, y = |Z| of shunt cap (with ESL + 0.75 nH/via) and bead Z(f); mark 1 Ω / 1000 Ω lines and the 30–1000 MHz stop band; and CM voltage at connector vs 100 µV.
10. **Crosstalk isolation** (ARCH-065): x = 10 MHz–1 GHz log, y = additional loss vs adjacent-trace case (−5 to 30 dB); curves for 1–4 empty tracks and guard trace.
11. **Differential mismatch** (ARCH-096, 097): SI sim; x = 0–3 GHz, y = CM current (dBµA) or CM voltage across 100 Ω (dB increase vs matched); curves 1, 2, 4, 20 % mismatch.
12. **Enclosure worst-case envelope** (ARCH-113): x = 0.3–2.1 GHz, y = E-field at 3 m (dBµV/m, −20 to 30) estimate vs measurement.
13. **Edge via fence** (ARCH-114): x = 100 MHz–10 GHz log, y = reduction in near E-field (−10 to 40 dB) per pitch.
14. **Gasket transfer impedance** (ARCH-112): inject current across seam, measure seam voltage; x = frequency, y = Z_t; reject SE-only datasheets.
15. **Lab triage** (ARCH-116 to 118): spectrum analyser at failing frequency; contact voltage probe scan of seams/connector pins/shield terminations; copper-tape confirmation; cable move/remove test with caveat.
16. **Heatsink voltage spectrum** (ARCH-029): spectrum analyser between heatsink and ground-reference plane, 0–2000 MHz; expect bus harmonics dominating.

## 5. Pitfalls, failure modes, review checklist

- Assuming even harmonics are absent (duty ≠ 50 % or t_r ≠ t_f creates them) — p.15.
- Confusing Mb/s with MHz: 100 Mb/s has 50 MHz fundamental — p.17, p.222.
- Leaving fast default edge rates "for timing convenience" — p.228 fn; only 5–7 harmonics needed — p.95.
- Analysing voltage only; current radiates — p.23, p.92, p.171.
- Treating "ground" as a zero-impedance sink; current returns to its source in a loop — p.43, p.55.
- Reserving 0 Ω / cap / ferrite pads for PCB-to-chassis bonds — p.20-21.
- Standoffs placed arbitrarily, far from I/O connectors — p.65.
- Thin/long standoff posts; too few connector tabs — p.54.
- Pigtail or drain-wire shield terminations — p.54, p.226.
- Using earth-safety ground as an HF reference — p.55.
- Single-point ground strategy above ~100 kHz — p.56.
- One or two heatsink ground contacts (creates resonance in clock-harmonic band) — p.63-64.
- High-speed trace crossing a plane split (any plane name) — p.71.
- Relying on one stitching capacitor (good only to ~100 MHz) or ignoring its 1.5 nH mounting inductance — p.74.
- Placing the stitching capacitor > 0.5 in from the crossing — p.100.
- Signal via that changes reference planes without adjacent return path; extra vias/caps > 0.5 in away — p.102.
- Layer pair (3,4) in a 6-layer or (3,9) in a 12-layer — p.189, 191.
- Card-to-card signal referenced to different plane nets on the two cards; return pins assigned by name instead of by referenced plane — p.80-83.
- HF traces within 0.5 in of the board edge — p.98.
- Exposed outer-layer segments > 1 cm on HS nets — p.95.
- Capacitors (instead of ferrites) across an I/O isolation split; split narrower than 50 mil; island without chassis contact; HS traces near LS I/O — p.108-110.
- Expecting a split to isolate above ~400–500 MHz — p.111.
- Power/ground planes extending under the LS I/O area or under post-CM-filter differential traces — p.112, 169.
- Crosstalk between layers with no plane between; cascaded crosstalk onto I/O — p.114-115.
- Guard trace vias farther apart than λ/10 — p.117.
- Mixing decoupling values (cross-resonance 75–200 MHz); "high-frequency" small caps — p.137-138.
- Adding more global caps to fix noise above ~500 MHz — p.136.
- Expecting local caps to lower remote plane noise (only ~5 dB at 50 mil) — p.142.
- Lossy caps near ICs or at via transitions — p.147-148.
- SPICE-only PDN analysis above ~200 MHz — p.135.
- Decaps on the far side of the board from the plane pair (long vias) — p.190.
- I/O filter > 1 cm from connector — p.152.
- Filter capacitor referenced to the wrong side of the I/O split (strategies #2, #3) — p.158-159.
- Shunt cap on the connector side as the only emissions element — p.156.
- Leaded capacitors in HF filters (+20–24 dB) — p.164.
- Choosing ferrites by Z@100 MHz only — p.166.
- Differential pairs routed across splits; assuming pairs immune — p.181.
- Non-conductive chassis finish under gaskets; smooth cloth gaskets; trusting dual-room SE data — p.210-212.
- Slot < λ/10 assumed sufficient — p.200.
- Vent webs < 25 % of hole diameter — p.209.
- Near-field H-probe used to locate leaks (misleading) — p.223-224.
- Assuming cable removal identifies the source (antenna change) — p.225.
- Spread-spectrum clock treated as a real amplitude reduction — p.222 fn.
- Loop-mode far-field (10 m) predictions from bare-board models — p.94.
